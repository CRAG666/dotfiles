# Notation and sources

How the literal reference text is marked in these files, and how to read its notation. Read this file before any other reference file.

## Contents

- Sources
- Notation
- Conventions when using the excerpts
- Misprints reproduced as printed

## Sources

The reference files hold literal text from three sources, under the headings `## Source A`, `## Source B`, and `## Source C`. Text under `## Additional phrases` or `## Skill rules` does not come from them. Section numbers and cross-references inside a source refer to that source only.

- **Source A:** phrases grouped by section of a paper and by function, notes on style, punctuation, articles, sentences, paragraphs and the writing process, and three lists.
- **Source B:** phrases with optional words and GB/US variants, writing help (style, punctuation, grammar, vocabulary, numbers, time), a thesaurus, and a glossary.
- **Source C:** rules of grammar, usage, and style, each with correct and incorrect examples.

## Notation

| In the reference files | In the original | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `*word*` | italics | Source B: a word from the Academic Word List, or a cross-reference in the thesaurus. Other sources: the author's own italics (a word under discussion, an example). |
| `**word**` | bold | The author's own emphasis. In Source C, the part of the example that the rule is about. |
| `[word]` in Source B | lighter print | An optional word or phrase; the markers *or*, *etc.*, GB and US between alternatives; an antonym in the thesaurus. |
| `\[word\]` | square brackets | Brackets printed in the original. |
| `~~text~~` in Source C | red print | An incorrect or less effective form. Never use it. |
| Source C example without `~~` | blue print | A correct form. |
| `<small>text</small>` in Source C | black print beside an example | The author's comment on the example, not part of the example. |
| X, Y, and ellipsis dots | the same | A slot for a name or for your own words. |
| Capital or small first letter in Source B | the same | A capital marks a way to begin a sentence; a small letter marks a phrase used inside a sentence. |
| `<br>` inside a table cell | stacked lines | Alternatives: choose one line per column and read across the row. |
| `> Screenshot (...)` in `translation.md` | a screenshot image | The text shown in the image, typed by hand. |

## Conventions when using the excerpts

- **Phrases are frames.** Fill the slots with the user's own content. A phrase never licenses a claim, an example, or a citation that the user did not supply.
- **Spelling.** Source A is converted to US spelling (*analyse* to *analyze*, *whilst* to *while*, *towards* to *toward*). Three places keep British forms: its list of commonly used verbs, which is given in British spelling on purpose; the entries where a British form is the subject (*practice/practise*); and one table cell that lists *While* and *Whilst* as alternatives. In Source B, *towards* is changed to *toward* in the chapter 8 phrases; where a line shows both forms, marked GB and US, output the US form. In Source C, *behaviour* is changed to *behavior* in two examples of section 17.21.
- **Names.** Where a source refers to itself by title, the excerpt says *this book* or *this document*; the author's surname in Source C's own examples is *Smith*.
- **Typography.** The excerpts keep their curly quotation marks, dashes, and ellipsis character. Output stays ASCII (rule 11 in `core-principles.md`).
- **Hand-typed content.** Two parts of Source C are images in the original and were typed by hand: the quantifier table in section 12.1 (`grammar-numbers-units.md`) and the screenshots in chapter 16 (`translation.md`).

## Misprints reproduced as printed

- Source C 7.10 (`grammar-numbers-units.md`): "Brazil is **biggest** than Argentina." and "The system performed **best / worst / least efficiently / most efficiently** in the first test than in the second test." are printed in blue, but they are the incorrect counterparts of the lines above them. Do not use them.
- Source B 10.2 (`glossary.md`): the rows *chlor* and *hypn* repeat the example words of the rows above them (*chiropractic, surgery*; *hyperactive, hypersensitive, hyperbole*). Ignore those examples.
